# Recruiter Outreach

**Project:** RecriVio – AI-Powered Recruitment Intelligence Platform

**Purpose:** This document contains professional outreach templates for recruiters, hiring managers, engineering leaders, founders, and HR professionals. The objective is to start genuine conversations around the project rather than immediately asking for referrals or jobs.

---

# Before You Send Any Message

A common mistake during job searches is treating outreach as a transaction.

```
Hi.

Can you refer me?

Here's my resume.

Thanks.
```

Recruiters receive hundreds of similar messages every week.

Instead, give them a reason to remember you.

The objective is not to ask for a referral.

The objective is to demonstrate that you've solved an interesting problem and would value their perspective.

Think relationship first.

Opportunity second.

---

# Outreach Framework

```
Research

↓

Personalization

↓

Context

↓

Value

↓

Conversation

↓

Relationship
```

Every message should answer four questions naturally.

• Why are you contacting this person?

• Why are they relevant?

• What have you built?

• Why should they spend two minutes replying?

# Message 1

## Recruiter Connection Request (LinkedIn)

Hi **{Recruiter Name}**,

I came across your work in talent acquisition at **{Company}** and noticed you're involved in hiring for engineering roles.

Over the last few months I've been building **RecriVio**, an AI-powered recruitment intelligence platform that explores recruitment analytics, hiring workflows, and executive dashboards from an engineering perspective.

Since recruitment is your domain, I thought you might find the idea interesting.

I'd love to connect and continue learning from professionals working in this space.

Thank you.

---

# Why It Works

✓ Personal

✓ Short

✓ No referral request

✓ Domain relevance

✓ Starts a conversation

# Message 2

## After Connection Is Accepted

Hi **{Recruiter Name}**,

Thank you for connecting.

One of the reasons I reached out is that I've been researching how modern recruitment platforms are evolving beyond traditional ATS functionality.

As part of that work I built **RecriVio**, which combines recruitment analytics, executive dashboards, AI-assisted resume intelligence and hiring insights into a single platform.

I'm not reaching out to ask for a referral.

I'd genuinely value your perspective as someone working directly in recruitment.

If you have ten minutes sometime, I'd appreciate any feedback on whether the platform reflects real recruitment challenges or where you think it could improve.

Either way, thank you for connecting.

---

# Why It Works

The message removes pressure.

People are much more willing to give advice than immediately recommend someone they don't know. Recruiters often respond better to genuine conversations than transactional requests.

# Message 3

## Recruiter After Applying

Hello **{Recruiter Name}**,

I recently applied for the **{Role Name}** position.

While reviewing your careers page, I noticed your team is investing in modern recruitment practices, which immediately caught my attention.

Coincidentally, I've been working on a research-driven project called **RecriVio**, focused on recruitment intelligence, hiring analytics, AI-assisted resume evaluation, and executive reporting.

Whether or not my application progresses, I'd be interested in hearing your thoughts on how engineering teams can build better tools for recruiters.

I've attached my portfolio and project repository below if you're interested.

Thank you for your time.

# Message 4

## Hiring Manager

Hello **{Hiring Manager}**,

I recently came across your engineering team while researching **{Company}**.

Rather than sending a generic introduction, I wanted to share one project that represents how I approach software development.

Instead of building another CRUD application, I spent several months studying recruitment workflows and designed an AI-powered recruitment intelligence platform called **RecriVio**.

The project combines software engineering, analytics, system architecture and business research into a single platform.

I believe projects become more valuable when they demonstrate decision-making rather than simply technical implementation.

If you have a few minutes, I'd genuinely appreciate any technical feedback.

Thank you.

Thank you for considering it.

# Message 7

## Asking for Feedback Instead of a Referral

Hi **{Name}**,

I'd like to ask for something slightly different than most LinkedIn messages.

I'm not looking for a referral.

I'm looking for an honest opinion.

I've been building a recruitment intelligence platform that combines AI, analytics and software engineering, and I'm currently refining the product before expanding it further.

If you had five minutes to review the repository or documentation, I'd genuinely value any critical feedback.

Fresh perspectives usually uncover things I've missed.

Thank you.

# Message 8

## After Receiving Feedback

Thank you for taking the time to review the project.

I appreciate both the positive comments and the constructive criticism.

Some of the points you've raised are already on my roadmap, while others have given me entirely new ideas to explore.

Feedback like this is one of the reasons I enjoy sharing projects publicly.

# What Recruiters Actually Notice

Based on current recruiter guidance and portfolio best practices, strong outreach is usually supported by a repository that clearly explains:

• The business problem

• Your individual contribution

• Technical architecture

• Documentation quality

• Live demo or visuals

• Measurable outcomes

Recruiters and hiring managers consistently emphasize that projects should tell a coherent story with context and impact, not just expose source code.

---

# Outreach Mistakes to Avoid

✗ Asking for a referral in the first message.

✗ Sending the same message to everyone.

✗ Writing long autobiographies.

✗ Using AI-generated buzzwords.

✗ Explaining every technology used.

✗ Attaching a resume before it's relevant.

✗ Following up every day.

✗ Treating networking as a transaction rather than a professional conversation.

---

# Personal Rule

A good outreach message should leave the other person thinking:

> "This person clearly invested time understanding a real business problem."

—not—

> "This is another candidate asking for a referral."

That difference is often what turns a cold message into the beginning of a professional relationship.
