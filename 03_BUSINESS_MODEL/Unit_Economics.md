 # Unit Economics – Recrivio

Version: v1.0
Research Type: Financial Systems Analysis
Confidence: Mixed (Fact + Industry Modeling + Strategic Inference)

---

# 1. Purpose

This document does not attempt to estimate Recrivio's financial statements.

Instead, it identifies the fundamental economic drivers that determine whether the business can scale sustainably.

The key question is:

"What is the smallest repeatable unit of value creation inside Recrivio?"

---

# 2. Defining the Economic Unit

In SaaS:
    Unit = One paying customer

In E-commerce:
    Unit = One order

In Ride Sharing:
    Unit = One completed ride

In Recruitment:

    Unit = One successfully completed hiring mandate.

Everything else exists to maximize the efficiency and profitability of that unit.

# 5. Time Economics

Every open mandate consumes recruiter capacity.

Example:

Recruiter handles:
- 8 roles → deep focus but low throughput
- 20 roles → balanced productivity
- 50 roles → quality degradation

The optimal point balances:
- Candidate quality
- Time-to-fill
- Revenue per recruiter

---

# 6. Throughput Model

Assume:

One recruiter closes:

12 successful hires/year

Revenue per placement:
₹X

Annual recruiter-generated revenue:

12 × ₹X

Increasing recruiter productivity from 12 to 18 hires/year can improve profitability without proportional headcount growth.

This is one of the strongest scaling levers.

# 8. The Hidden Bottleneck

Many assume sourcing is hardest.

Hypothesis:

The real bottleneck is converting sourced candidates into successful joins.

Drop-offs occur because of:
- Better competing offers
- Delayed interview cycles
- Salary mismatch
- Poor candidate experience
- Changing business priorities

Reducing these losses has disproportionate financial impact.

---

# 9. CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)

Components may include:

- Sales salaries
- Founder selling time
- Marketing
- Travel
- Events
- Proposal preparation
- Business development

Formula:

CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Spend
      --------------------------------
      Number of New Clients Acquired

Objective:

LTV / CAC should be comfortably greater than 1, ideally much higher.

---

# 10. LTV (Lifetime Value)

Weak Client:

1 hiring mandate
→ relationship ends

Strong Client:

Year 1:
5 hires

Year 2:
8 hires

Year 3:
Payroll support
Compliance support
Strategic hiring

Total value compounds over time.

Retention therefore has major economic significance.

---

# 11. Gross Margin Dynamics

Revenue grows when:

- Placements increase
- Recruiter productivity improves
- Automation reduces manual effort
- Repeat business increases

Margins shrink when:

- Recruiters remain idle
- Hiring freezes occur
- CAC rises
- Clients churn

---

# 12. Operational Leverage

Bad scaling:

Revenue ↑ 50%
Employees ↑ 50%

Excellent scaling:

Revenue ↑ 50%
Employees ↑ 15%

Technology and automation determine which trajectory dominates.

# 16. Biggest Strategic Risk

The business can become people-constrained.

If every incremental ₹1 of revenue requires proportional recruiter hiring, scaling efficiency remains limited.

The long-term moat therefore depends on increasing revenue per recruiter through process optimization and technology.

---

# 17. My Founder-Level Hypothesis

Recrivio's long-term value is unlikely to come from being "another recruitment agency."

Its durable advantage would emerge if it evolves into a Talent Intelligence Platform where:

- proprietary data,
- workflow automation,
- recruiter expertise,
- AI-assisted decision support,
- and client trust

combine to produce hiring outcomes faster and more accurately than competitors.

That transformation shifts the business from labor-intensive services toward technology-enabled operating leverage.
