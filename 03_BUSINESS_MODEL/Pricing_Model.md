# Executive Summary

Pricing is not merely a mechanism for charging customers.

It is a strategic instrument that determines:
- Revenue predictability
- Cash-flow stability
- Client acquisition speed
- Market positioning
- Risk allocation
- Long-term profitability

For Recrivio, pricing should be viewed as an optimization problem balancing customer affordability with sustainable delivery economics.

---

# 1. Pricing Philosophy

The client is not purchasing resumes.

The client is purchasing:

- Reduced hiring time
- Reduced execution risk
- Access to specialized talent
- Recruiter expertise
- Market intelligence
- Business acceleration

Therefore pricing should align with value created rather than effort invested.

---

# 2. Possible Commercial Models

## Model A — Success Fee

Client pays only after successful joining.

Flow:

Requirement
    │
    ▼
Search
    │
    ▼
Interview
    │
    ▼
Selection
    │
    ▼
Joining
    │
    ▼
Invoice

Advantages:
- Low entry barrier
- Easy client acquisition
- Strong alignment

Disadvantages:
- Revenue uncertainty
- Cash-flow volatility
- Delivery risk borne by recruiter

Suitability:
High

---

## Model B — Retained Search

Client pays upfront engagement fee.

Typical flow:

Contract
    │
    ▼
Advance Payment
    │
    ▼
Dedicated Search
    │
    ▼
Hiring
    │
    ▼
Final Settlement

Advantages:
- Predictable cash flow
- Strong commitment
- Better planning

Disadvantages:
- Harder enterprise sales
- Higher customer friction

Suitability:
Medium

---

## Model C — Recruitment Process Outsourcing (RPO)

Client outsources multiple hiring mandates.

Revenue:

Monthly Retainer
        +
Performance Incentives

Characteristics:

- Recurring revenue
- Longer contracts
- Better recruiter planning
- Higher customer lifetime value

Suitability:
Very High

---

## Model D — Employer of Record (EOR)

Potential structure:

Monthly Fee
      +
Compliance Fee
      +
Payroll Management

Recurring and operationally stable.

- Customer Support
- Operations

Medium:
- Frontend Engineers
- QA

Hard:
- AI Engineers
- Staff Engineers
- DevOps Architects
- Security Specialists

---

# 5. Enterprise Discount Logic

Large clients may receive:

- Volume discounts
- Multi-role agreements
- Annual contracts
- Preferred pricing
- Dedicated recruiter allocation

Objective:
Increase long-term revenue rather than maximize single-deal margin.

---

# 6. Strategic Pricing Objective

Bad Strategy:

Maximize fee per hire.

Better Strategy:

Maximize lifetime customer value.

A lower-margin client that hires continuously can outperform a one-time premium engagement.

---

# 7. Risk Allocation

Question:

Who bears hiring risk?

Success Fee:
Recruiter bears more risk.

Retainer:
Client shares risk.

RPO:
Risk distributed over long-term partnership.

The pricing structure implicitly defines incentive alignment.

---

# 8. Competitive Pricing Position

Competing only on price creates a race to the bottom.

Instead, differentiation should come from:

- Speed
- Candidate quality
- Technical understanding
- Data-driven sourcing
- Relationship quality
- Market intelligence

Premium positioning requires premium outcomes.

# 11. Negotiation Strategy

Clients negotiate on:
- Price
- Guarantee period
- Replacement policy
- Speed
- Candidate quality

Winning negotiations should rely on demonstrable value rather than aggressive discounting.

---

# 12. Hidden Pricing Asset

The strongest pricing power does not come from contracts.

It comes from reputation.

If clients believe Recrivio consistently delivers better hiring outcomes, willingness to pay increases and price sensitivity declines.

---

# 13. CEO-Level Observation

The optimal pricing strategy is one that maximizes:

Expected Customer Lifetime Profit

rather than

Immediate Transaction Revenue.

Long-term partnerships create higher predictability, stronger referrals, lower acquisition costs, and greater operational leverage.
